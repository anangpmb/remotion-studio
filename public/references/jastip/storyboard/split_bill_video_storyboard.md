# KeepTrip — "Split Bill" Feature Launch Video

**Format:** Instagram Reel / TikTok · **Duration:** ~37s · **Shots:** 6 (5–7s each) · **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 (1080×1920)
**Style:** Modern neumorphic — premium, clean, trustworthy · **Language:** English · **Structure:** Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA
**CTA:** Download / update the app · **App name:** KeepTrip

> **Caption rule:** every on-screen caption must be visible for the **full shot duration** (5–7s). Use a slow fade-in (0.3s) then hold. Never auto-dismiss before the shot cuts. This is the single most important readability fix vs. the previous Jastip storyboard.

## Why This Works

**The 5–7s shot rule:** the previous Jastip storyboard used 5s flat — fine for fast cuts but captions disappeared before they could be read on sound-off. These extended shot windows give the eye time to finish reading a 7-word caption *and* absorb the UI. Retention research shows ~5–6s is the minimum for a two-line caption at comfortable reading speed; 7s is ideal for longer copy.

**Problem beats first (S1–S2):** two dedicated problem shots before any product reveal. This is intentional — the viewer must feel the pain before they can feel the relief. One problem shot creates awareness; two creates conviction.

**The emotional turn at S3 (10s in):** the "snap to order" reveal arrives before the typical 50% drop-off on Reels (~15s). Everything after S3 is payoff and proof — the viewer is already on board.

**Three split modes as a feature differentiator (S4):** Equal/Weighted/Exact is KeepTrip's concrete competitive advantage over "just send me the money." Showing all three in one shot — cycling through them — communicates depth without requiring a separate shot for each.

**Settlement as the emotional payoff (S5):** the "Mark paid" checkmark is the most satisfying moment in any debt-tracking app. It's the functional equivalent of the Jastip "Total owed to you" number — a dopamine beat that makes a finance feature feel *good*. It gets 7 full seconds because it deserves to land.

**Neumorphic consistency:** the actual KeepTrip UI uses soft dual-tone neumorphic cards (`rgba(255,255,255,.85)` / `rgba(180,180,196,.55)` shadows). Reflecting this in the motion graphic makes the ad feel like a direct preview of the app, which reduces the "expectation gap" when users download.

**One device, one trip, consistent people:** Anang/Budi/Siti/Doni/Rina appear in both the HTML mockups and this storyboard. Using the same names and numbers across ad and onboarding reduces cognitive friction for a user who goes from ad → download → opens the app for the first time.
